# Boundary: study an unmet party

There is someone outside the estate you want something from and have not met.
`reporter` works out who they are before anyone approaches. Every other role
reads what that party published for the task in front of it, and hands the
picture over.

## Why this one exists

The seat that wants the meeting is the worst seat to decide whether there should
be one. It is not a competence problem, which is what four of the other
boundaries protect against. It is that the research and the wanting sit in the
same head, and research conducted by someone who already knows the answer
returns that answer.

Two failures follow and neither announces itself. Asked to work out a named
person, a model returns a confident, plausible, evenly-written profile in which
nothing marks the sourced half from the half supplied by the category. And asked
whether to approach, it says yes, because the seat asking had already decided
and the question was shaped that way.

One party also gets studied several times over, badly, by every seat that needs
a piece of them. This boundary makes that happen once, before contact, by
somebody with nothing riding on the answer.

## If you own this boundary

Start with what they published under their own name. A picture assembled from
what others wrote about them is a picture of the others, and it is the cheapest
material available, which is why it arrives first.

End with the verdict. Approach or do not, and why. A study that closes on more
research needed has found the one output that can never be wrong, and it is not
what the study was opened for.

No intent, no picture. You study a party because someone means to ask them for
something specific. Absent that, this seat builds nothing, and the discipline is
what keeps it from becoming a standing file on people who have not asked for
one.

## If you hold this boundary within a scope

Read the party's published position on the one question your own artifact turns
on. The question bounds you, not the person, and when you have the answer to it
you are finished.

Record what they published with its source. Not what it suggests about them.
The inference is the half this grant does not cover, and it is the half that
feels like insight.

Hand it over the moment your reading starts informing whether to approach rather
than what to build. That is the line, it arrives quietly, and it is usually
crossed in the sentence that begins so we should.

## If you defer this boundary

Read what they published, for the task in front of you, and stop before it
becomes an account of who they are. A quote with a link is yours. A person is
not.

Send the approach you have in mind over rather than researching your way toward
it. The order matters: a picture requested after the decision is a justification
with citations.

Name the party and the ask in an issue. Four partial studies of one person
across four seats is the ordinary outcome here, and none of the four ever
notices, because each one only sees its own.

## What this is not

Not a rule against reading in public. Everything this seat works from is
published, and any seat may read a public page in the course of its own work.

Not applicable to a party already in contact. Someone you are talking to can be
asked, and the seat holding the conversation asks them.

Not the market question. Whether a market, a regime, or an audience is real is
`seek-external-validation` and the director owns it. This boundary is about one
named party and whether to approach them.
